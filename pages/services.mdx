Two kinds of clients, two different sets of constraints — the same underlying discipline. Below is
what that looks like for each, plus what carries over regardless of who the site is for.

<Section heading="For storefronts & small businesses">
<CardGrid minWidth="260px">
<Card kicker="Design & Build" title="A site that converts">
A homepage built to turn a browsing visitor into someone who shows up, books, or buys — not a
portfolio piece for the client to admire and never update.
</Card>
<Card kicker="Speed" title="Fast on real phones">
Built and tested for the phones your actual customers carry, not the newest device on the market.
</Card>
<Card kicker="Handoff" title="You own it">
Clear documentation and a component system your next hire (or the studio, on retainer) can extend
without starting over.
</Card>
</CardGrid>
</Section>

<Section heading="For public agencies & contractors">
<CardGrid minWidth="260px">
<Card kicker="Compliance" title="WCAG 2.2 AA, documented">
Accessibility conformance built in from the start, with the documentation an RFP review actually
asks for — not a checklist added at the end.
</Card>
<Card kicker="Content" title="Plain-language forms">
Application and permit flows written for a first-time visitor with a real task, not administrative
phrasing carried over from a paper form.
</Card>
<Card kicker="Continuity" title="Survives staff turnover">
A maintenance story and a documented component system, so the site doesn't become unmaintainable
the day the person who commissioned it moves on.
</Card>
</CardGrid>
</Section>

<Section heading="What carries over either way">
The same design tokens, the same restraint about when an animation earns its place and when it
doesn't, and a fixed-scope quote before any work starts — see [Pricing](/pricing) for
what that looks like, or [get in touch](/contact) to talk through your project.
</Section>
