It's tempting to put the orbiting shapes and the glitch text on every page once you've built them — they're fun, they're on-brand, and they took real effort to get right. Almost none of that effort is a good reason to use them everywhere.

<PullQuote>Not every page needs the full effects budget — a survey of when to dial it back.</PullQuote>

A login screen is a single, brand-forward moment — there's nothing to read yet, so spending the whole effects budget there costs nothing. A pricing page is the opposite: the entire job is legibility and trust, and a headline that tears itself apart mid-sentence works directly against that job, no matter how good it looks in isolation.

The test we keep coming back to: would a first-time visitor with a real task — pay a bill, compare two plans, find a form — be slowed down by this? If yes, it doesn't belong on that page, however good it is.
