Most weeks we're working on at least one project for a small storefront business and one for a public agency or contractor, often at the same time. The two briefs almost never overlap, but the discipline underneath them is the same: figure out what the page actually has to do before deciding what it gets to look like.

<PullQuote>What changes when the same studio ships a farmers-market storefront and a county parks site in the same quarter.</PullQuote>

A farmers-market site needs to convert a browsing visitor into someone who shows up Saturday morning. A parks-department site needs a resident who's never filed a permit application before to find the right form without calling anyone. Neither of those goals is served by a homepage that's trying to impress.

What carries over is the underlying system — the same tokens, the same restraint about when to spend an animation budget and when not to. The brand stays recognizable. The volume knob just moves a lot.
