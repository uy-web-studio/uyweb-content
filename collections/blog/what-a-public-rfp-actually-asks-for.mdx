Public-sector RFPs read very differently from a small-business brief, but they're asking for the same underlying thing: proof that the site will still work for the person who needs it most, not just the person reviewing the pitch.

<PullQuote>Notes from a few years of responding to municipal and agency web contracts.</PullQuote>

That usually means accessibility conformance documentation, a plain-language content plan, and a maintenance story that survives staff turnover — none of which show up in a typical storefront brief, and all of which change what "good" looks like for that page.

The work is still design work. It just gets measured by a different, and often stricter, bar.
