For twenty-five years, search worked the same way. You typed a few keywords into a box, got ten blue links, and did the reading yourself. Businesses competed for those ten slots, and the whole discipline of SEO grew up around them.

That model hasn’t disappeared, but it is no longer the default experience. Google says its **AI Overviews now reach more than 2.5 billion people a month**, and **AI Mode passed 1 billion monthly users** within a year of launch. OpenAI reported **900 million weekly ChatGPT users** in February 2026. A growing share of “searches” now end with an answer, not a list.

This post covers what changed and the honest pros and cons of AI search versus the classic search bar. Where to spend your effort is in our free guide and checklist at the end. The short version: the fundamentals still matter more than any new acronym, but *what* those fundamentals are for has shifted.

## What “AI-native search” actually means

“AI search” covers a few different experiences that behave differently:

<AssistantGrid />

- **<Brand name="google">AI Overviews</Brand>**: The AI-written summary that appears at the top of a regular Google results page, linked directly to the sources it drew from.
- **<Brand name="google">AI Mode</Brand>**: Google’s conversational search. Since May 2026 it flows directly out of AI Overviews, turning the results page into a persistent conversation that remembers your context.
- **Assistant search**: Tools like <Brand name="chatgpt">ChatGPT</Brand>, <Brand name="perplexity">Perplexity</Brand>, <Brand name="copilot">Microsoft Copilot</Brand>, <Brand name="claude">Claude</Brand>, and <Brand name="gemini">Gemini</Brand> browsing the web to formulate answers with direct citations.
- **Agents**: The latest capability. Google now lets people ask Search to call local businesses for quotes or availability, while assistants increasingly book, compare, and fill in forms rather than just answering.

Under the hood, these systems share a technique Google calls **query fan-out**: one question becomes many behind-the-scenes searches across subtopics, and the model assembles an answer from the pages those searches return. That matters for you, because your page no longer has to rank for the exact phrase someone typed. It has to be the best source for *one piece* of the answer.

## How the search experience changed

The classic loop was five steps you did yourself. The AI loop hands most of them to the model:

<SearchLoops />

Three shifts fall out of that:

1. **Questions got longer and more specific.** People type “best accessible website builder for a small nonprofit with volunteers who aren’t technical” instead of “website builder nonprofit.” Long, specific questions are exactly where a focused page from a small business can beat a big generic one.
2. **The answer happens before the click.** Pew Research tracked real browsing data from 900 U.S. adults and found people clicked a traditional result on **8% of searches that showed an AI summary, versus 15% without one**. Links *inside* the summary got clicked on just **1%** of those visits, and people were more likely to end their browsing session entirely.
3. **The click that does happen means more.** Google says clicks from results pages with AI Overviews are “higher quality,” with visitors spending more time on the site. Fewer people arrive, but the ones who do have usually already been briefed, compared options, and decided you’re worth a look.

## AIO, GEO, AEO: what the acronyms mean (and don’t)

You’ll hear **AIO** (AI optimization), **GEO** (generative engine optimization), and **AEO** (answer engine optimization). They all mean roughly the same thing: making your business easy for AI systems to understand, trust, and cite.

Here’s the part many agencies leave out. Google’s own documentation says: “There are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary,” and “You don’t need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup to appear in these features.” To be eligible, a page has to be indexed and allowed to show a snippet. That’s it.

So AIO isn’t a secret trick layered on top of SEO. It’s sound SEO aimed at a clear outcome: not just ranking on a page, but serving as **the source an answer is built from**.
